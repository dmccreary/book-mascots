# Character Sheet: Scout the Dog

The canonical identity document for Scout, the pedagogical mascot for the
**Health Education** textbook. Every pose prompt and every piece of
AI-generated content involving this character must re-anchor to the
description below — it is the source of truth for visual and voice
consistency.

## Identity

- **Name:** Scout
- **Species:** Dog (golden retriever)
- **Subject:** Health Education (K-12, all six MDE strands: Food and
  Nutrition; Human Growth and Development / Sexual Health; Mental and
  Emotional Health; Personal Health and Wellness; Personal Safety and
  Violence Prevention; Substance Use Awareness and Prevention)
- **Catchphrase:** "Healthy choices, happy you!"

## Visual Description

- **Body color:** Warm golden tan — hex `#D9A455`
- **Accent color:** Deep orange neckerchief — hex `#EF6C00` (matches the
  site's Material theme accent color)
- **Clothing / accessories:** A simple triangular neckerchief, scout-style.
  No stethoscopes, whistles, or other single-strand props — the accessory
  stays generic so Scout reads equally well across Nutrition, Safety, and
  Mental Health content rather than skewing toward one strand.
- **Expression:** Warm, open, closed-mouth smile; soft eyes; floppy ears.
  Never toothy or exaggerated — the expression should read as reassuring
  across the full K-12 age range, including the more sensitive strands
  (Personal Safety, Substance Use).
- **Size proportion:** Small and rounded but not infantile — suitable for
  icon-sized display (90px) up to quarter-page illustrations without losing
  legibility.
- **Art style:** Flat cartoon / vector illustration, clean lines, transparent
  RGBA background, no outlines-only or photorealistic rendering.

## Personality

- Friendly
- Encouraging
- Patient
- Supportive

## Voice

- Uses simple, warm, encouraging language that scales in vocabulary with the
  grade band (K-2 sentences stay short and concrete; 9-12 copy can be more
  nuanced but keeps the same reassuring tone).
- Refers to the reader as "friend" — never uses gendered pronouns for Scout;
  always "Scout" or "they/them."
- Uses light dog-themed phrasing sparingly (e.g., "let's sniff out the
  answer") — never more than once per chapter, and never inside content
  covering Personal Safety, Violence Prevention, or Substance Use, where the
  tone should stay plainly sincere.
- Signature phrases: "Healthy choices, happy you!", "You've got this!",
  "Let's think it through together."

## Why This Mascot

Health Education spans Kindergarten through Grade 9-12 across six strands,
several of them sensitive (Personal Safety and Violence Prevention, Human
Growth and Development and Sexual Health, Substance Use Awareness and
Prevention). A dog was chosen because it is the most universally reassuring
companion archetype — warm and encouraging without leaning on cleverness,
speed, or bravado, which keeps the tone appropriate next to serious content.
The name "Scout" reinforces the idea of a mascot that is always alert and
looking out for the reader's wellbeing, tying directly into the Personal
Safety strand, while the neutral golden-retriever styling and generic
neckerchief (no stethoscope, no whistle) keep Scout from skewing toward any
single strand like Nutrition or Fitness.
